# Narration (LinkedIn #14: "3 filters before you automate"), from Tony's own comment reply (Charles thread), 2026-11-09

Tony's words, lightly adapted for voice. The insurance example is left out (it's in #12). Dark UI style, like ai-analyst-reel.
```
How do you decide what's safe to automate with AI?

I use three filters.

One. Does it touch a soul? Prayer, care, counsel. For these, a person always handles it.

Two. What does a wrong answer cost? If it's cheap to undo, automate it. If it's costly, and not just financially costly, but reputation costly, keep a human in the loop.

Three. Is the AI at least as good as the person? Most people skip this one.

A human in the loop isn't error free either. The goal isn't to build a perfect AI. It's to build one that matches or outperforms a typical hire.

Measure how often a given step fails when it's done by a person. Someone you would actually hire. Then run the AI on the same cases, and compare the results. There are statistical tests for this that most people ignore.

If the AI performs as well as or better than the human hire, automate it. If not, keep the human in the loop, and keep improving.
```
